## Section A — Audience segmentation (the 6 segments + 1 amplifier)

You named 6 audience segments plus an Awwwards-tier credibility group. Not all are addressable in month 1. Most are not. Below is the honest matrix: what each segment is, how many you can reach, what they pay for, and whether to lead with them.

### Segment matrix

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Why these specific picks for "primary"

**Vibecoder designers (Segment 2):** This segment didn't exist 2 years ago. They use v0/Lovable/Bolt to ship UIs but care about design quality. Their pain: AI-generated UIs all look the same — generic shadcn-flavored gray-and-white components. Toolskin's exact pitch fits: "AI generates the code, your design system makes it look like *yours*." This is the most underserved high-intent segment in 2026 and Toolskin's positioning is tailor-made for it.

**Frontend WP/CMS devs (Segment 4):** This is your existing audience and your fastest revenue. WP devs are accustomed to paying for premium themes ($59-299/yr is normal — see Avada, Astra Pro, GeneratePress Premium, Kadence Pro). They have technical literacy to install Toolskin. They don't trust AI generators (different workflow, different deliverable expectations). They have agency clients who need consistent design. **They will pay; the others won't yet.**

**Awwwards-tier designers (Segment 7):** They don't pay you, but if 2-3 of them retweet a Toolskin token-swap demo with "this is unhinged in a good way," every other segment treats you as legitimate. This is the credibility play. Cost: zero. Effort: occasional comments and engagement on their work.

### Why I'm pushing the others to "defer"

Not because the segments aren't valuable. Because **solo, in 30 days, you cannot pursue all 6 simultaneously and execute well on any of them**. Picking 2 primaries plus 1 amplifier is already aggressive for solo capacity.

If month 1 hits the base case ($1.5-3K), you expand to Segment 1 (designers no-code) in month 2 with bundle products. If month 1 hits aggressive ($4K+), you expand to Segment 3 (vibecoder devs) in month 2 with a free starter kit. Segments 5 and 6 are months 4+ territory, possibly never if WP and Vibecoder paths grow.

## Section B — Realistic reach math (per segment)

The headline numbers in the original deck assumed you could "do 50 cold DMs per week." This is sustainable for B2B sales reps with proper tooling. For a solo founder also doing build, marketing, support, and admin: **realistic outreach budget is 50-100 *quality* outreach actions per week, not 200.** Quality > volume always, and inflated volume produces lower conversion rates because you can't research properly.

### Weekly outreach allocation (realistic for solo)

| Activity | Hours/week | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Vibecoder designer outreach (Twitter DMs + replies on demos) | 4-6 | 20-30 contacts |
| WP dev outreach (LinkedIn + WP Slacks + cold email) | 5-7 | 25-40 contacts |
| Awwwards-tier engagement (comments, replies, never DMs) | 2-3 | 10-20 interactions |
| Content production (Twitter posts + 1 blog/week + 1 video) | 8-12 | ~5 tweets + 1 long-form piece |
| Existing-audience nurture (Twitter, email replies) | 2-4 | Ongoing |
| **Total marketing/sales hours** | **21-32 hrs/week** | **~80-120 quality contacts/month** |

That leaves ~10-20 hrs/week for build, support, and project delivery. **Which means you can sustainably deliver 1 active service project at a time without sales pipeline dying.** Two simultaneous projects = pipeline dries up = no revenue in month 2-3.

### Funnel projections (calibrated, not industry-baseline)

**Month 1 funnel for Vibecoder designer + WP dev segments combined:**

```
Outreach actions sent (quality, researched):    ~80-120
  ↓ Reply rate (conservative, no case studies):  ~3-5%
Replies received:                                ~3-6
  ↓ Reply-to-call rate (no portfolio):           ~25-35%
Calls held:                                      ~1-2
  ↓ Call-to-close rate (limited proof of work):  ~15-25%
Service contracts closed:                        ~0.2-0.5
```

This means you should expect **0-1 service contracts in month 1**. Closing 1 is success. Closing 2 is excellent. Zero is normal and recoverable.

**Bundle sales funnel from inbound (content + organic discovery):**

```
Twitter impressions (200 followers, 5 tweets/wk): ~5,000-15,000/mo
  ↓ Click-through to site:                        ~1-2%
Site visits from Twitter:                         ~50-300
  ↓ ProductHunt launch day spike:                 ~500-3,000
  ↓ HackerNews if it lands:                       ~0-10,000
Total month-1 site visits:                        ~600-13,000
  ↓ Conversion rate (paid digital products):      ~1-2%
Bundle sales:                                     ~6-260
```

The huge spread is because of HackerNews variance. Without HN: 6-30 bundle sales. With HN front-page: up to 260. Plan for the no-HN case; treat HN as upside.

### Recalibrated 30-day revenue scenarios

| Scenario | Probability | Service revenue | Bundle revenue | Total cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Floor (worst case) | ~25% | $0 | $0-$300 | **$0-$300** |
| Base case (most likely) | ~50% | $1,500-3,000 (1 contract) | $300-1,000 | **$1,800-$4,000** |
| Stretch (above-average) | ~20% | $3,000-6,000 (1-2 contracts) | $1,000-3,000 | **$4,000-$9,000** |
| Lucky (HN/PH front page + early referral) | ~5% | $5,000-10,000 | $3,000-15,000 | **$8,000-$25,000** |

**Plan around the base case.** The point of this exercise is not to convince your partner that aggressive is likely — it's to set expectations so the partner doesn't shut you down at $2K when $2K was the realistic floor anyway.

## Section C — Workforce reality (the hardest section to write)

Solo founder + 30 days + 6 audience segments + multi-stream business is not achievable. You have to cut. Here's what's possible and what's not.

### Hours/week budget at solo capacity

A working week realistically has 50-60 productive hours when you're hustling. After essentials (sleep, food, basic life), 70-80 hrs is technically possible for short bursts (2-4 weeks max) before burnout. Don't budget on burst capacity.

### Solo allocation — base case (sustainable)

| Activity | Hrs/week | % of week |
|---|---|---|
| Sales outreach + follow-up | 10-12 | 22% |
| Content production (writing, video, posts) | 10-14 | 26% |
| Service delivery (1 active project max) | 15-25 | 36% |
| Build / product improvements | 5-8 | 11% |
| Support, admin, comms | 3-5 | 5% |
| **Total** | **45-55 hrs** | **100%** |

This is sustainable for 12-16 weeks. After that, burnout risk spikes. **By month 4, you must either be hitting numbers that justify hiring help, or pivot.** This is the hard timeline gate.

### What gets cut in this allocation

- ❌ Multi-channel content (Twitter only — no LinkedIn presence, no Instagram, no TikTok)
- ❌ Paid ads (you don't have time to manage them well)
- ❌ Active community building (Discord, forums) — too time-intensive
- ❌ Multiple service offerings simultaneously — pick 1-2 anchor offers
- ❌ More than 2 audience segments in primary outreach
- ❌ Conferences, podcasts, partnerships in month 1

### Workforce expansion thresholds

| When | What to add | Hours/week added | Cost/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MRR > $3K stable for 2 months** | Part-time VA for outreach research + admin | +10-15 hrs | $400-800 |
| **MRR > $7K stable for 2 months** | Designer/dev contractor for service delivery | +15-25 hrs | $1,500-3,000 |
| **MRR > $15K stable for 3 months** | Full-time partner or co-founder | +40 hrs | Equity or $4-6K/mo |

Don't hire before these thresholds. The temptation will be strong. Resist.

## Section D — Marketing moves required to hit base case

Here's the specific weekly cadence required to make the funnel math actually deliver. If you skip these, the numbers don't work — they're not aspirational, they're the input variables.

### Weekly marketing cadence (solo, base case)

**Twitter/X (your primary channel):**
- 5 posts/week (mix: token-swap demo videos, hot takes on design system trends, build-in-public progress)
- 2 threads/week (longer-form: technical breakdowns, before/after, philosophy)
- Daily engagement: 30 minutes replying to other accounts in your space, never spam-pitch
- Goal: grow to 1,000 followers by month 3

**LinkedIn (for WP dev segment):**
- 2 posts/week (case-study format: "How I rebuilt this WordPress site's design system in 4 hours")
- 10 connection requests/week to WP devs/agency owners with personal note
- Engage in Slack groups (Post Status, GenerateBlocks, etc.)

**Content publishing:**
- 1 blog post/week on `toolskin.org/blog` (technical: "How OKLCH auto-contrast actually works", "Why your design system needs a token engine")
- 1 video/week (short, < 90 seconds, focused on token-swap or before/after)
- Submit best post each week to dev.to and Hashnode

**Specific launch moments (one-time efforts):**
- Week 1: Soft launch on personal Twitter
- Week 2: ProductHunt launch (Tuesday-Thursday is best)
- Week 3: HackerNews "Show HN" submission
- Week 4: First case study published (from W2-3 service delivery)

**Outreach (50-70 quality contacts/week):**
- Monday: Research 25 prospects (mix of vibecoder designers + WP devs)
- Tue-Wed: First-touch outreach (DM/email)
- Thu: Follow-ups on previous week's non-responses
- Fri: Engagement-based outreach (comment-then-DM strategy)

### What "good outreach" actually looks like (template)

Bad outreach (don't do):
> "Hey! I built a CSS framework called Toolskin that I think you'd love. It's like Tailwind but better. Want to check it out?"

Good outreach (do this):
> "Saw your [specific recent project / tweet / post]. The way you handled [specific design choice] caught my eye.
>
> I built something that might fit your workflow — Toolskin, a token-driven design engine where one CSS variable retones the whole system. Probably nothing, but felt worth a 90-second look since you clearly care about [specific thing they care about].
>
> [link]
>
> No reply needed unless interested."

The ratio difference between these two messages is roughly 5-10× in reply rate. Quality > volume, every time.

### Domain note: `toolskin.org` strategy

`.org` is the right call for an open-source-led project. It signals "infrastructure, not SaaS land-grab." Tailwind is `.com` because they are SaaS. Next.js is `nextjs.org` because they are framework. Toolskin should follow Next's lead.

Implementation:
- `toolskin.org` — main site, docs, free download
- `toolskin.org/pro` or `pro.toolskin.org` — paid product (when launched)
- `toolskin.org/agency` or similar — service inquiries
- `toolskin.org/showcase` — gallery of sites built with Toolskin

## Section E — Case studies (extracting credibility from what you have)

You have 4 mockups: Higgsfield, YouTube Studio, Suno, and the userscript toolpane work. These are NOT delivered client work, but they are **demonstrable proof of design system capability**. Used right, they function as case studies even though no client paid for them.

### How to convert mockups to case-study credibility

For each mockup, build a public case study page on `toolskin.org/showcase/[name]`:

**Structure for each:**

1. **Hero:** The before/after (or alternative versions, since these are mockups). High-quality screenshots.
2. **The problem:** "[Tool name]'s native UI looks like [characterization]. Power users want [thing]."
3. **The Toolskin solution:** Side-by-side: native UI vs Toolskin-styled version. Token map showing which `--ts-*` variables drive the new design.
4. **Technical detail:** "Built in 4 hours using only Toolskin tokens. Zero custom CSS. Re-themable to any brand color in one variable change."
5. **Live demo:** Embedded interactive token swap (orange → teal → red live).
6. **Disclaimer (be upfront):** "This is a design exploration, not an official product collaboration. Toolskin is open-source; modifications welcome."

The disclaimer is critical. Pretending these are paid client work is lying and will be caught. **Owning that they're "design explorations" is a strength, not a weakness** — it positions you as someone who builds for the love of the craft, which the Awwwards-tier audience respects more than agency client logos.

### What this gives you in service sales conversations

> "Have you done client work with Toolskin?"
>
> "Not yet — Toolskin v1 is just shipping. What I have is design system explorations on real-world UI surfaces: Higgsfield, YouTube Studio, Suno toolpanes. Each rebuilt entirely from token primitives in under 4 hours. You can see the live demos at toolskin.org/showcase. **You'd be the first paid project — which means you get founding-customer pricing and direct access to me as the system's author.**"

The "founding customer" framing turns no-case-studies into a feature. Use it for the first 3-5 service contracts, then drop it once you have real delivered work.

### Bonus: the Claude Design experiments as case studies

The dev-ide demo, ops dashboard, and marketing hero you generated with Claude Design are also case-study fuel. Frame them as: "Here's what AI generates against Toolskin's token system. Notice how it stays on-brand even though no human styled these — because the tokens drive everything." This is exactly the pitch that resonates with the Vibecoder Designer segment.

## Section I — The conversation to have with your partner

After they read this, the conversation needs to land on three agreements:

**1. We agree what month-1 success looks like.**
> "Success in 30 days is: 1 signed service contract OR $2K cumulative revenue, plus measurable pipeline (5+ active conversations). NOT $30K. NOT vanity metrics."

**2. We agree what month-3 success looks like.**
> "Success in 90 days is: $5K cumulative OR clear path to $5K MRR (e.g., 3+ recurring service clients OR consistent bundle sales). If we miss this, we have a clear conversation about pivot or wind-down."

**3. We agree the kill-switches.**
> "If at day 14 we have zero booked calls, we audit. If at day 30 we're below $1.5K cumulative, we discuss. If at day 60 we're below $5K, we decide together. If at day 90 we're below $15K, we wind down or restructure with new commitments."

The agreement is the deliverable. Not the projections. Numbers will be wrong; partnerships fail when commitment misalignment isn't surfaced. By naming the kill-switches up front, you're inviting your partner into honest conversation, which is what makes them want to back you for longer.
